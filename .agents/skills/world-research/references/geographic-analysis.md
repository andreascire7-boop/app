## Administrative units

Always state which unit a figure applies to — they are not interchangeable and mixing them silently produces wrong comparisons:

- **City / municipality**: the legal administrative unit. Population and area figures here are usually much smaller than "the city" colloquially means.
- **Urban area / agglomeration**: the built-up continuous area, may span multiple municipalities.
- **Metropolitan area**: the municipality plus its commuter zone — usually the largest and most-quoted population figure, and the most different from the municipal one (e.g. a "city" population can be 5-10x smaller than its metro population).
- **Region / province / state**: sub-national administrative division, relevant for taxes, healthcare systems, and some cost-of-living data.
- **Country**: national-level data — needed for currency, visa rules, national holidays, and country-wide statistics (GDP, national indices).

When citing population, cost, or crime data, name the unit explicitly: "Vienna (municipality, 2024): ..." not just "Vienna: ...".

## Distance vs. travel time

Three different numbers — never substitute one for another:

1. **Straight-line (great-circle) distance** — useful only for flight-time rough estimates, meaningless for road/rail planning.
2. **Road/rail distance** — actual route distance; varies by mode and route choice.
3. **Travel time** — depends on mode, traffic/schedule, transfers, and time of day. State the mode when giving a time (e.g. "2h10m by direct train" vs. "3h30m by car in typical traffic").

For any "how far / how long" question, give travel time by the traveler's actual mode, not just distance.

## Coordinates, elevation, climate zone

Include when relevant to the question (outdoor activity, climate comparison, altitude sickness, etc.):
- Coordinates (lat/long) for precise location and to disambiguate same-named places
- Elevation — affects climate, temperature, and physical exertion
- Köppen climate classification or equivalent, when comparing climates structurally rather than just citing temperatures

## Airport-city mismatches

Do not assume an airport is located in, or convenient to, the city it's named after or nominally serves. Always verify the airport's actual municipality and its distance/transfer time to the destination — some "city" airports are 60-100+ km away and served by long bus transfers, not rapid transit. Check the airport's own site and the destination's transit authority rather than assuming.

## Source priority order

Search and cite in this order, preferring higher-priority sources whenever available:

1. **Official primary sources**: government portals, municipal/city government sites, airport authority sites, airline/rail operator sites, official tourism boards
2. **National statistics offices**: national statistical institutes, central banks (for exchange rates/inflation)
3. **International statistical bodies**: Eurostat, World Bank, OECD, UN agencies, IMF
4. **Reputable research/data providers**: established market research firms, industry data providers with transparent methodology
5. **High-quality journalism / specialist publications**: established outlets with a track record on the topic, specialist trade press
6. **Community sources** (forums, wikis, crowdsourced cost databases): use only as supplementary color or to cross-check, never as the sole basis for a decision-critical figure — their methodology and recency are usually unverifiable

If only lower-priority sources are available for a decision-critical figure, say so explicitly and mark the figure `UNVERIFIED` or `ESTIMATE` rather than presenting it with unwarranted confidence.
