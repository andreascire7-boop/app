## Research categories

For any relocation question, cover the categories the user cares about from: housing, utilities, food, transport, taxes, salaries/employment market, visa/residency requirements, healthcare access and cost, safety, language, climate, infrastructure, social/community life, and overall quality of life. Use [cost-of-living.md](cost-of-living.md) for the cost categories specifically.

## "Cheap" vs. "good value"

Always distinguish these — conflating them is the most common relocation-research mistake:
- **Cheap** = low nominal cost of living.
- **Good value** = cost relative to what it buys: local salary levels, healthcare quality/access, safety, infrastructure, and services. A place can be cheap and low-value (low cost but also low services/safety/opportunity) or expensive and high-value.

When answering "should I move to X", address both explicitly rather than defaulting to "cheap = good".

## Visa/residency rule

**Never assume visa or immigration eligibility based on general knowledge.** Rules vary by the person's nationality, purpose (work/study/retirement/investment), and change over time. For any visa/residency question:
1. Identify the specific visa/residency category relevant to the user's stated purpose and nationality.
2. Search the destination's official government immigration/consular source for that category.
3. State the requirements found, the source, and the date checked.
4. If the user's nationality or exact situation isn't specified, ask for it rather than assuming — eligibility genuinely depends on it.
5. Explicitly recommend confirming with the relevant consulate/embassy or a licensed immigration professional before acting, since requirements can change and edge cases are common.
