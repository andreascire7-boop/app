# Country comparison methodology

Uses the same process as [city-comparison.md](city-comparison.md) (table → weights → weighted score → verdict categories → sensitivity check), with country-level additions below.

## Country-level additions

- **Use national statistics, not capital-city figures**: cost of living, wages, and quality-of-life data for "the country" must come from national sources, not from the capital or largest city standing in for the whole country — call this out if the only available data is capital-only.
- **Currency and purchasing power**: convert to a common currency at a stated exchange-rate date, and note when purchasing-power differences (not just nominal prices) matter more than raw currency conversion — e.g. for cost-of-living or salary comparisons, consider citing a purchasing-power-adjusted figure alongside the nominal one if the source provides it.
- **Regulatory/legal differences**: taxes, healthcare system structure, visa/residency regimes, and labor law differ nationally — these are not comparable on a 0-10 "vibe" score; describe them factually and let the user weigh them, or fold them into the weighting only if the user gave a clear preference.
- **Regional variation within a country**: flag when a national average hides large internal variation (e.g. cost of living, safety, or climate differing sharply between regions) rather than presenting one number as representative of the whole country.

## Default weights

Same defaults as city comparison unless the user's question is really about national policy dimensions (taxes, healthcare, visas) — in that case, ask which of those matter most rather than guessing a weight for something with no natural "score".
