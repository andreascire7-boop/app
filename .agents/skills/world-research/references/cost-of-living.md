# Cost of living research

## Contents
- Category breakdown

## Category breakdown

Always separate cost of living into distinct categories rather than a single blended number — a single "cost of living index" hides which categories actually drive the difference:

- Rent (state bedroom count/area and neighborhood tier — city-center vs. outside-center numbers differ substantially)
- Utilities (electricity, heating/cooling, water, internet)
- Groceries
- Restaurants/eating out
- Local transport (public transit pass, fuel, ride-hailing)
- Entertainment/leisure
- Healthcare (out-of-pocket vs. insured cost, and whether public healthcare is accessible)
- Taxes (income tax bracket relevant to the user's situation, VAT/sales tax, property tax if relevant)
- Childcare/education, if the user has dependents
- Any other category the user specifically asked about

## Source guidance

- Prefer current local sources: national statistics offices, local real-estate listing sites (for actual current rents, not historical averages), local utility providers, local government tax pages.
- Crowdsourced cost indices (e.g. user-submitted cost databases) are useful for a quick sanity-check range but should be labeled `ESTIMATE` at best — their sample sizes and recency vary a lot by city, and they should never be the sole source for a decision-critical number. Cross-check against at least one local/official source when the figure matters.
- For rent specifically, current listings beat any index — listings show what's actually on the market now.

## Normalization steps

1. Convert all figures to one currency at a stated exchange-rate date.
2. State the household assumption (single person, couple, family + size) consistently across cities/countries being compared.
3. Match the neighborhood tier (city-center vs. outside-center) — comparing a city-center rent in one city to an outer-suburb rent in another silently biases the result.
4. Separate "cheap" (low nominal cost) from "good value" (cost relative to income/quality/services) — see [relocation.md](relocation.md) for that distinction in a relocation context.
