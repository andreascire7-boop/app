## Dimensions to research

Never answer a safety question with a single "safe" or "dangerous" label. Research and report separately, to the extent relevant to the question:

- Crime types and rates relevant to a visitor/resident (violent crime vs. petty theft/pickpocketing are very different concerns)
- Common tourist scams specific to that destination
- Nightlife/late-night safety specifically, if relevant to the traveler's plans
- Public transport safety, including at off-peak/late hours
- Official government travel advisories for the traveler's home country, if one exists — cite the specific advisory level and date
- Any current exceptional events (unrest, natural disaster, disease outbreak, major disruption) that would override normal baseline safety information

## Neighborhood/context variation

Safety varies significantly within a single city — a national or city-wide statistic can mask large neighborhood-level differences. When the question is about a specific area/neighborhood or a specific activity (e.g. walking alone at night), research at that level rather than citing only a city-wide crime rate.

## Sources

- Official government travel advisories (the traveler's own government's foreign-travel advisory service)
- Local police/government crime statistics where published
- Reputable current journalism for recent/exceptional events
- Community sources (travel forums) only as supplementary color on scams/local specifics, cross-checked against at least one higher-priority source for anything decision-critical (see priority order in [geographic-analysis.md](geographic-analysis.md))
