## Historical averages vs. forecast

Keep these strictly separate and label which one you're giving:

- **Historical climate averages**: long-term monthly/seasonal averages (temperature, precipitation, sunshine hours) used to answer "what's the weather usually like in [month]" or "best time to visit". These are stable and don't need re-searching every time, but should still cite a source and note the averaging period (e.g. "1991-2020 average").
- **Current/forecast weather**: only reliable roughly 10-14 days out; anything beyond that is a seasonal outlook at best, not a forecast. Never present a seasonal outlook as if it were a specific-day forecast.

Never answer a "what will the weather be like" question for a date far in the future using anything but historical averages, clearly labeled as such — do not imply forecast-level confidence.

## Sources

- National meteorological services (official, authoritative for both historical data and forecasts)
- Köppen-Geiger climate classification for structural climate comparisons (e.g. comparing climate *type*, not just average temperature)
- For live/near-term forecasts: the national meteorological service or a reputable weather service, checked at the time of the question — state the check date since forecasts change.

## Best-time-to-visit answers

Base these on historical averages for temperature/precipitation plus, when relevant, seasonal factors the user cares about: tourist crowd levels, local pricing seasonality, and any relevant events/holidays. State the trade-off explicitly (e.g. "shoulder season: milder crowds and lower prices, slightly higher rain chance") rather than naming a single "best" month as fact.
