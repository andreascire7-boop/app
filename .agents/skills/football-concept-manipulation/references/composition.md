## Composition elements to establish

Before generation, define:
- Hero subject and focal point (almost always the player)
- Camera angle and perspective
- Foreground / midground / background — what occupies each, and why
- Leading lines and negative space
- Scale relationships
- Visual hierarchy — what's read first, second, third

Every major element needs a narrative purpose; if an element doesn't support the hero subject or the concept, cut it.

## Camera approach

Pick the approach that fits the concept rather than defaulting to one look:

- **Low-angle monumental** — subject towers over camera; use for dominance/scale concepts
- **Wide cinematic** — establishes environment and subject together; use for storytelling/context concepts
- **Telephoto compression** — flattens depth, isolates subject against a compressed background; use for tension/focus
- **Overhead** — reveals pattern, scale, or symbolic layout; use sparingly, for graphic/symbolic concepts
- **Symmetrical frontal** — formal, editorial, poster-like; use for identity/statement concepts
- **Dynamic diagonal** — energy, motion, instability; use for action/intensity concepts
- **Close-up editorial** — emotion, detail, texture; use for intimate/character-driven concepts

State focal length/perspective look, camera height, framing, depth of field, and subject distance explicitly in the art direction brief before generating.

## Dramatic scale

Use scale deliberately when the concept calls for it (giant player, miniature stadium, monumental trophy, enormous architecture, surreal scale relationships) — and make it believable, not just big:

- Consistent perspective and vanishing points across all elements
- Shadows and contact shadows that match the implied scale
- Atmospheric haze/depth cues that increase with distance
- Reference objects in the frame that anchor the scale relationship
- Depth of field consistent with the implied camera distance

## Format fit

Compose with the delivery format in mind from the start (see [social-formats.md](social-formats.md)) — hero placement, headroom, and safe margins differ between a square/portrait social crop and a wide presentation layout. Don't compose for one aspect ratio and crop after the fact if the format is already known.
