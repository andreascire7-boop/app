## Principle

Don't illustrate the concept as text or a literal scene — make the image itself communicate the idea. A strong football editorial graphic reads as a single visual statement, understandable without a caption.

## Translating an idea into an image

For an abstract concept, ask: what physical scene or scale relationship makes this idea visible? Some recurring approaches:

- **Dominance/scale** — a monumental player above a city or stadium
- **Overlooking/command** — the player physically positioned above or surveying the arena
- **Legacy/achievement** — a trophy reimagined as architecture (a monument, a throne, a gate)
- **Rivalry/hierarchy** — opponents represented at a diminished scale or distance, without being demeaning or defamatory
- **Territory/identity** — the stadium or city reimagined as a symbolic kingdom, fortress, or arena tied to the concept (e.g. Colosseum for a gladiator concept)
- **Transformation/journey** — environment or costume elements that reference the concept's narrative (era, myth, story world) while keeping the player and kit intact per fidelity rules

## Worked example

Concept: *"Player dominates the league."*

Weak (literal/text-based): a player standing next to the words "League Champion."

Strong (visual metaphor): the player rendered at monumental scale standing over a miniature stadium at dusk, floodlights at his feet like a crown, rival crests scattered small across the pitch below — the idea of dominance is *seen*, not stated. Combine this with dramatic scale technique from [composition.md](composition.md) and a matched lighting system from [cinematic-lighting.md](cinematic-lighting.md) so the scale reads as believable rather than collaged.
