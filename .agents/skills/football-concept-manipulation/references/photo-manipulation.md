## Goal

The result should read as an expert editorial photo composite — not an obvious AI image and not a cutout pasted over a background. Every technique below exists to make the player look physically present in the environment.

## Realistic compositing checklist

- **Shadows** — the player casts a shadow consistent with the scene's light direction and softness; contact shadow exists wherever the player touches a surface.
- **Reflections** — add them where the environment logically produces them (wet ground, glass, metal) and match them to the player's pose and the scene's lighting.
- **Edge integration** — edges of the subject pick up the color/light of the environment (no hard, flat cutout edges); fine detail (hair, fabric edges) integrates rather than sitting as a flat silhouette.
- **Color consistency** — subject and environment share a coherent grade; no mismatched white balance between subject and background.
- **Depth and atmospheric perspective** — elements further from camera lose contrast/saturation and shift toward the ambient light color, consistent with distance.
- **Perspective consistency** — vanishing points, horizon line, and camera height are consistent between subject and environment.
- **Lighting interaction** — see [cinematic-lighting.md](cinematic-lighting.md); subject and environment must share one lighting system.
- **Texture consistency** — grain, sharpness, and resolution level match across subject and environment; don't combine a crisp studio-lit subject with a soft/blurry background inconsistently unless depth of field explains it.

## Avoiding the "pasted-in" look

The most common tell of a weak composite is a subject that looks like it was cut out and dropped onto an unrelated photo. Check specifically for: mismatched shadow direction, missing contact shadow, mismatched color temperature, a halo/fringe around the subject's edge, and a subject that's sharper or softer than its surroundings with no depth-of-field justification. Fix these before considering the piece finished.

## Anti-generic-AI checklist

Before finalizing, check for and remove any of the following unless a specific one is deliberately justified by the concept:

- [ ] Generic, unspecific stadium background with no relation to the concept
- [ ] Random smoke/sparks/particles with no source or narrative reason
- [ ] Excessive lens flares
- [ ] Generic neon glow disconnected from the concept
- [ ] Fake/generic crowd filler with no purpose
- [ ] Plastic-looking or over-smoothed skin
- [ ] Distorted anatomy or incorrect hands
- [ ] Fabricated jersey details, sponsors, or names not backed by a reference
- [ ] Excessive HDR/over-sharpening
- [ ] Glow or light effects with no logical light source

Every effect in the final image should trace back to a reason established in the art direction — if it can't be justified, cut it.
