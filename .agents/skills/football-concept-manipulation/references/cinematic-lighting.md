# Cinematic lighting & color

## Lighting research

Research an appropriate lighting reference when the concept calls for a specific mood (e.g. sunset backlight, stadium floodlights, moonlight, dramatic volumetric light) rather than defaulting to flat, generic illumination. Real photographic or cinematographic references are useful here for direction/quality even though the final piece must be original.

## What to match

The player and the environment must share **one** lighting system — this is often the single biggest realism factor:

- Light direction (where shadows fall, consistently, across every element)
- Intensity and contrast ratio
- Color temperature (warm/cool consistent across subject and scene)
- Shadow softness/hardness matching the implied light source size and distance
- Rim/backlight, where used, consistent with a real light source position
- Reflected/bounce light from nearby surfaces onto the subject
- Ambient/environmental illumination (e.g. stadium glow, fire glow, moonlight) affecting both subject and scene

## Color palette

Build a coherent palette from: club identity colors, the competition/context (if the user specified one), the concept's mood, and the environment. Let this palette drive grading, atmosphere color, and background elements — but never use it to arbitrarily recolor the player's kit. The original kit colors must stay recognizable regardless of the surrounding grade; adjust the *scene's* palette to work with the kit, not the other way around.
